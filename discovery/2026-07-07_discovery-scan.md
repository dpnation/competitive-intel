# Discovery Scan — 2026-07-07

## Summary
Five candidates surfaced this week, four solid matches concentrated in accessibility testing (DevAlly, QualiBooth, TestParty) and crowdtesting (Testeum) — accessibility compliance funding is clearly heating up ahead of the EU Accessibility Act. The most notable is DevAlly, a Dublin-based Startup Battlefield alum that raised a €2M pre-seed (TechCrunch, Oct 2025) explicitly combining human expertise with AI/LLMs for continuous accessibility testing.

## Candidates Found

### DevAlly
**Match type:** funding
**Why it matches:** Audits websites for accessibility barriers (e.g. missing captions) and explicitly combines human expertise with AI/LLMs for continuous, scalable accessibility testing — direct overlap with Applause's accessibility testing service and Fable/Level Access's space. Targets companies needing EU Accessibility Act (effective June 2025) compliance.
**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/09/startup-battlefield-alum-devally-raises-2m-to-help-companies-with-europes-feisty-new-accessibility-law/)

### QualiBooth
**Match type:** funding
**Why it matches:** Spanish/European digital accessibility platform offering real-time scanning, monitoring, and audits (1,000+ audits performed) to help brands meet ADA/EAA/WCAG compliance — matches accessibility testing as a service, targeting retailers and enterprise B2C brands directly. Secured €2M to expand into the US.
**Source:** [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2025/07/08/qualibooth-secures-2m-to-accelerate-european-expansion-as-accessibility-legislation-takes-effect/)

### TestParty
**Match type:** funding
**Why it matches:** AI platform for accessibility testing, remediation, training, and compliance monitoring for websites — fits the specialized accessibility-testing-as-a-service criteria. Raised a $4M seed (Harlem Capital, Urban Innovation Fund, Bain Capital Ventures Scout Fund).
**Source:** [AlleyWatch](https://www.alleywatch.com/2024/07/testparty-ai-powered-accessibility-testing-platform-for-websites-compliance-monitoring-michael-bervell/)

### Testeum
**Match type:** review-platform
**Why it matches:** SaaS crowdtesting platform connecting curated global testers to software/app/website publishers, with an option for Testeum's own team to manage test strategy and execution — a direct crowdtesting-marketplace model like Applause's core category. Based in Australia/New Caledonia.
**Source:** [Testeum](https://testeum.com/)

### LMArena
**Match type:** funding
**Why it matches:** Raised $150M (Jan 2026, $1.7B valuation) for a human-preference-based AI model evaluation platform — relevant to Applause's Gen AI evaluation services in that it's a human-in-the-loop LLM eval model, though its buyers are AI labs/model builders rather than enterprise B2C digital teams. Flagging as a category signal rather than a direct competitor; worth monitoring if it moves toward enterprise application-layer eval.
**Source:** [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/lmarena-raises-150-million-to-build-the-worlds-most-trusted-ai-evaluation-platform-302653012.html)

## Notes
- Accessibility testing/compliance is the clear hot spot this week — three of five candidates (DevAlly, QualiBooth, TestParty) are accessibility-focused, likely driven by the EU Accessibility Act (effective June 2025) creating compliance urgency. Worth watching whether any of these show up in deals against Fable or Level Access.
- Screened out and excluded as pure automation tooling (no managed service/human layer): Snowglobe (AI chatbot simulation, synthetic personas, Guardrails AI), Arato (AI testing via simulated users, $10M seed), and most ProductHunt "Testing and QA" launches (QA.tech, Quash, AgenticQA, LangWatch, QApilot, Osloq) — these are dev-facing automation tools, not services.
- Also screened out: Yrikka (AI red-teaming, but narrowly defense/computer-vision focused, $1.5M pre-seed — not enterprise B2C fit) and Eye-Able (€20M raise, but an accessibility overlay/widget product rather than a testing service).
- eSSENTIAL Accessibility (KKR's "Accessibility-as-a-Service" investment) appears to already be under the Level Access brand ("eA") — treated as already covered by the existing Level Access entry in competitors.json, not a new candidate.
