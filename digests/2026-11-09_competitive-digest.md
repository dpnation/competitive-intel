## Competitor Updates

### Mercor
**Signal level:** 🟡 Medium (direct competitor, rated up one level)
**URLs checked:** mercor.com/blog (fetched), PLE post (fetched), web search (extended plus standard)
**Changes noted:**
- Nov 3, 2026: Released Physical-Engineering's Last Exam (PLE) with Google Cloud AI Research: 85 long-horizon tasks across mechanical, electrical, civil and structural engineering. Licensed engineers from Mercor's network wrote and validated tasks (44 expert-authored; Google built 41 through an adversarial loop). Best agent configuration scored 48.3%; top agent met 91% of individual constraints but only 41% cleared every hard check. Open benchmark with 29 public tasks and a hidden test set.
- Blog index also lists, not read in full: Nov 5 "Playing well with others: Putting AI in the org chart" (research), Oct 29 "Offline Evaluations in Practice: Improving Mercor's SRE Agent" (enterprise), Oct 27 "Introducing TaskNet: A New Taxonomy of Work" (research).
- Why it matters: a named Google partnership extends Mercor's benchmark franchise (APEX) from office work into physical engineering, and the Oct 29 enterprise post shows Mercor publishing evaluation practice aimed at enterprise deployers, which is Applause's buyer. Expert validation by licensed professionals is the proof point to watch against Applause's real-world testing and domain-expertise claims.
- Not new: the reported $20B round and Nvidia interest remain unannounced. Search for "Mercor" also returned Mercer International (unrelated).

### Innodata
**Signal level:** 🔴 High (direct competitor, rated up one level)
**URLs checked:** Q3 2026 press release via webdisclosure.com (fetched, full text); Motley Fool call transcript (fetched, not read in full); web search (extended plus standard, plus CEO check)
**Changes noted:**
- Nov 5, 2026 Q3 release (primary source): revenue $95.6M, up 53% year over year and about $12.3M above consensus; adjusted EBITDA $17.7M (18.5%); net income $10.3M; cash $164.0M. FY26 revenue growth guidance raised to 42% or more from 40% or more, "even after absorbing the wind-down of a program at one of our large customers" expected to be replaced by new programs in Q1.
- Customer diversification: a customer under 5% of revenue a year ago became the largest in Q3, with an additional Q4 program of about $18M already signed. Five new programs with a large hyperscaler (about $3.1M trailing-12-month revenue; more than $20M forecast from it in 2027). Five new logos in Q3 including model builders and an enterprise data platform.
- Expansion areas: physical AI (motion capture lab launched in September; pilots with frontier and robotics labs; one pilot, if successful, about $7M in 2027 revenue); enterprise AI (new relationships with a global hardware company and a major financial services firm); Federal (verbal win for a contract worth $5M a year for five years, expected to sign by end of Q4).
- Leadership: Rahul Singhal's first earnings report as CEO. Per secondary coverage of the Q2 release, the transition was announced in August with Singhal effective Sept 30 and founder Jack Abuhoff becoming Executive Chairman; not previously in baseline.
- The release does not mention the reported Oct 26 India and Philippines layoffs, and no company statement was found. The release does reference the program wind-down, which is consistent with, but does not confirm, the "ramp-down of the business assignment" language in the reported termination emails. Secondary sources cite a Q4 revenue impact of about $12M and an analyst price-target cut (Craig-Hallum to $75 from $120); neither verified.
- The release gives no revenue figure for the Evaluation and Observability Platform; its boilerplate now frames Innodata as providing "the data, evaluation frameworks, and human expertise required to build AI systems that can be trusted at scale."
- Why it matters: the concentration concern from last week is partly answered (a second customer of comparable scale), but Innodata is now explicitly selling into enterprise AI, financial services and physical AI, and its evaluation and trust messaging overlaps Applause's. Sales should not cite the layoffs externally.
