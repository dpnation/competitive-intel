Overview

What the Battlecard Builder does

The Battlecard Builder is a Claude skill that generates formatted, print-ready competitive battlecards. You describe what you need — a specific competitor, a solution area, or a company you've never heard of — and it handles the research, structures the content, pulls the latest competitive intel from our weekly site scan, and outputs a Word document ready to share with reps.

It lives inside Claude Enterprise. Anyone on the team with the skill installed can trigger it with a plain-language request.

📄

On-demand generation

Trigger a card at any time — before a big deal, after a competitor makes a move, or when a rep asks "what do we have on X?"

⚡

Live intel baked in

Automatically fetches our weekly competitive scan and surfaces the most recent findings at the top of every card.

🔍

Auto-researches unknowns

If you ask about a company not in our corpus, it web-searches them, classifies their solution area, and builds a card from scratch.

🗺️

Solution context included

For any competitor, it can overlay the relevant solution-area landscape — who else plays there and how Applause is positioned.

Usage

How to use it — step by step

The skill is conversational. You don't fill out a form — you just describe what you need. Here's the typical flow:

1

Open Claude and trigger the skill

Start a conversation in Claude Enterprise with a plain-language request. The skill recognizes battlecard-related requests automatically. You can be as specific or as open-ended as you like.

Example prompts "Build a battlecard for Testlio"
"Build an accessibility testing battlecard"
"I keep hearing about Evinced — can we build a card on them?"
"Refresh the Fable battlecard with the latest Cowork scan"
2

The skill fetches fresh intel

Before generating anything, the skill automatically fetches our weekly competitive scan at dpnation.github.io/competitive-intel — pulling all reports from the last 6 months. It extracts signal-level findings (🔴 High / 🟡 Medium / 🟢 Low) for the target competitor and maps them to the card.

For unknown competitors, it also web-searches their homepage, LinkedIn, recent press, and any analyst mentions at this stage.

3

Review and confirm the content plan

The skill will show you what it's working with before generating — flagging what it found, what's uncertain, and any fields that need your input. You can redirect or add context here before it writes the card.

4

Download the .docx

The output is a formatted Word document — landscape orientation, Applause brand colors, all standard battlecard sections (Offensive table, Defensive table, Company Overview, Win Stories, Call to Action). Open in Word and Export → PDF if needed for sharing.

Note: Always review cards generated from web research before sending to reps. Fields marked [unverified] or [TBD] need a human check first.
Card types

Three card formats, one skill

The skill automatically picks the right format based on your request. Here's what each one produces:

Competitor card

One specific company

Full profile on a single competitor — their pitch, weaknesses, how to beat them, and how to handle their strengths.

  • Company overview
  • Offensive table (4–6 rows)
  • Defensive table (3–4 rows)
  • Win stories
  • Discovery questions
  • CTA for reps
Solution card

A testing domain

Landscape view of all competitors in a solution area — accessibility, payments, crowdtesting, AI testing, etc.

  • Industry players table
  • Category-level offense
  • Category-level defense
  • Applause's position
  • Key differentiators
Hybrid card

Unknown competitor

Researched from scratch. Combines a competitor profile with solution-area context so reps have the full picture.

  • Web-researched profile
  • Solution area landscape
  • Confidence markers
  • Cowork recommendation
Intel feed

How the competitive scan feeds every card

We run an automated weekly scan every Monday that monitors 10+ competitors across messaging, product launches, blog posts, analyst recognition, and press releases. Results publish to our competitive intel site and are automatically pulled into every card the skill generates.

The intel appears at the top of the content column as a high-visibility table — signal level, scan date, and finding — so reps know exactly what's changed before they walk into a call.

⚡ COMPETITIVE INTEL — LATEST UPDATES
🔴 High May 18, 2026 AI Chatbot Testing launched April 16 — human-led, 4-domain risk framework. Hallmark+ among first enterprise adopters.
🔴 High May 18, 2026 CEO keynoted STAREAST: "What Does Quality Mean for AI Testing?" Press release: "Testlio Accelerates Growth as AI Testing Demand Surges."
⚠️ New May 11, 2026 Baseline scan established. Homepage JS-SPA — content via search and PR coverage only.

Tracked competitors include: Testlio, Testbirds, UserTesting, Qualitest, QualityLogic, Test IO (EPAM), Global App Testing, UNGUESS, Level Access, and Fable. Signal levels: 🔴 High = material move requiring card update; 🟡 Medium = notable but not urgent; 🟢 Low = no change.

Unknown competitors

What happens when we don't have intel

If a rep mentions a company that isn't in our corpus — or you spot a new name in a deal — the skill doesn't stop. It automatically runs a research flow:

1

Web research the company

Searches their homepage, LinkedIn, recent press, G2/Capterra reviews, and any analyst mentions. Populates all standard card fields from public sources.

2

Scan the full Cowork archive

Checks all historical scan reports for any passing mention of the company — even in cross-competitor themes or footnotes.

3

Classify the solution area

Determines where they compete (accessibility, payments, crowdtesting, AI testing, etc.) and pulls in the relevant solution-area landscape automatically.

4

Build a hybrid card

Outputs a full competitor card plus a "How [Company] fits the [Solution Area] landscape" section — giving reps both the specific intel and the broader competitive context. All unverified fields are flagged clearly. The skill also recommends adding the company to the weekly scan going forward.

Tips

Getting the most out of it

🎯
Be specific about solution context. "Build a Testlio battlecard for an accessibility testing conversation" gives you a card with both the Testlio-specific intel and the accessibility landscape overlaid — more useful than a generic card.
🔄
Refresh after 🔴 High signal weeks. When the weekly scan surfaces a major competitor move — a product launch, acquisition, or messaging shift — trigger a card refresh. The skill will flag exactly what changed and where it affects positioning.
✏️
Win stories need you. The card generates everything it can from available data, but win/loss stories have to come from the field. Use the TBD placeholder as a prompt to fill in a real example — even one strong story changes how reps use the card.
🔍
Unknown ≠ unimportant. If a rep keeps mentioning a company you've never tracked, that's a signal. Ask the skill to research and build a card — and add them to the Cowork scan so future cards get live intel instead of web research.
⚠️
Review [unverified] fields before field use. Cards generated from web research are clearly marked. The content is useful as a starting point, but treat it as a draft until a human has confirmed the key facts — especially revenue, employee count, and customer claims.
📬
Nominate wins and losses. The Call to Action on every card asks reps to submit wins and losses to Sales Enablement. That feedback loop is what keeps the cards honest and sharp over time.
